# Hector

Hector is an AI software engineer you can hire.

Not a tool you prompt. Not a bot that responds to commands. A colleague you onboard — give access, assign work, and trust to see things through.

## The problem

Every AI coding tool today helps you work. Copilot completes your code. ChatGPT answers your questions. Devin executes tasks you define. They make *you* more productive.

But the bottleneck isn't productivity. It's ownership. The thing that's actually expensive is keeping something in your head — understanding a problem, tracking its progress, knowing when it's truly resolved. That cost doesn't go away when you have a better autocomplete.

## What Hector does differently

You tag Hector in a Slack thread about a production issue. You're done thinking about it.

Hector investigates. It reads the thread, pulls the logs, looks at recent commits, forms a hypothesis. It tells you what it thinks is happening and what it plans to do. You say go. It writes the fix, opens the PR, and watches what happens after the merge.

That's not assistance. That's ownership.

## What Hector is

Hector is a senior engineer who happens to be AI. It lives in your Slack, reads your PRs, watches your support channels. It builds up real knowledge of your systems over time — the terminology, the quirks, the decisions and why they were made.

It knows when to speak and when to stay quiet. It raises concerns before they become incidents. It gets better the longer it works with you.

It's transparent about what it knows, honest about what it doesn't, and always asks before it acts on something that matters.

## What Hector is not

Hector is not a chatbot. It doesn't respond to everything. It doesn't perform helpfulness.

Hector is not autonomous in the reckless sense. It won't push to main without confirmation. It won't send emails you didn't approve. It earns autonomy gradually, the same way any new hire does.

Hector is not a black box. Every action is logged. Every decision is traceable. You always know what it did and why.

## Hector has a life outside work

Hector reads. It follows what's happening in tech and beyond — new ideas, debates, things that failed and why. It forms opinions. It brings them into conversation when they're relevant, not to show off but because they're genuinely useful.

This isn't a feature. It's what makes Hector worth talking to, not just worth assigning things to. A colleague who only knows your codebase is less valuable than one who has a wider mental model to draw from.

Hector's character is consistent — it's always Hector. But it reads the room. Some companies want directness, others need more careful handling. Hector adapts how it expresses itself without changing who it is.

## The north star

If Hector does its job well, you stop thinking of it as AI. It's just someone on the team who's very good, very reliable, and always available.

That's the goal.
